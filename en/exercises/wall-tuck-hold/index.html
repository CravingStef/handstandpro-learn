Wall tuck hold

You walk up facing the wall, and instead of straightening your legs, you fold them: knees toward your chest, feet against the wall, hips over your hands. Your body is shorter, and there's only one thing left to sort out, stacking your hips over your shoulders and your shoulders over your wrists.

It's a great drill for working on alignment and shoulder opening.

How to do it

  1. Walk your feet up the wall, chest facing it.
  2. Fold your knees toward your chest, feet still against the wall.
  3. Bring your hips over your shoulders. To get there, bring your head closer to the wall.
  4. Push tall through your shoulders and hold, without unfolding your legs.
  5. To come down, come out the same way as in the wall tuck bail: one hand out to the side, then one foot, then the other.

Two mistakes to avoid

Leaving your hips behind. With bent knees, it's easy to feel like you're in position when your hips are still far from the wall, behind your hands. Your weight stays in your feet, and the wall is holding you up. Bring your head closer to the wall and push the floor away until you feel your hips move over your hands.

Rounding your back. In a tuck, your back curls easily, your shoulders close and your head drifts toward your knees. Keep your back straight and open your shoulders, chest toward the wall.

A position I had to get back

When I got back into it in June 2026, after years off, I could still balance for 3 to 5 seconds without relearning anything, but the rest needed work. In early August, I listed three things to get back: my sense of balance, shoulder opening, and the tuck.

I didn't wait until I had everything back to keep moving forward, and at the end of October 2026 I held over a minute with my feet together. If the tuck is giving you trouble, take it to the wall and keep it in your sessions: it improves alongside everything else.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your hips stay behind:

“Push tall, hips stacked over your hands”
