Full hold (feet together)

The handstand as you picture it: legs together from start to finish, body stacked from hands to feet. It's the measure people mean when they quote a time, and it's the hardest one.

How to do it

  1. From a lunge, place your hands and kick one leg up, without forcing.
  2. Bring the other leg up to meet it, and squeeze your feet together.
  3. Push the floor away, grow tall, and keep your hips over your shoulders.
  4. Correct with your fingers, in small presses.
  5. Cartwheel out when you go past vertical.

The two mistakes that cost the most

Piking at the hips. As the feet come together, people often bend slightly at the hips to keep their balance. The hold lasts, but it's no longer a line. Stay tall, glutes squeezed, and accept a shorter hold that's straight.

Correcting too hard. An overdone correction sends your body the other way. The second one chases the first, and you fall by the third. Corrections come from small presses of the fingers, not from the hips.

A full minute, in 2023

I held a full minute with my feet together on 15 February 2023, at 36, after three months of shoulder strengthening. After years off, I started again in June 2026, held 57 seconds on 17 September, then over a minute on 27 October 2026, at 40.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, as your feet come together:

“Stay tall, do not pike at your hips”
