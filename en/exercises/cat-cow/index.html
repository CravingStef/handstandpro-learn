Cat-cow flow

A yoga movement, forty seconds on all fours. In the app, it ends the warm-up in the alignment and core sessions, before pike compressions and the hollow body hold.

The link to the handstand is more direct than it looks. The cat's rounded back is your pelvis tilting backward and your ribs tucking in: the base of the hollow body, the one that keeps you from ending up in a banana upside down. The cow, back arched, is exactly the position to avoid. Moving from one to the other teaches you to feel the difference, and which way to correct.

How to do it

  1. On all fours, hands under your shoulders, knees under your hips.
  2. Breathe in as you arch your back: your belly drops, your chest and gaze rise.
  3. Breathe out as you round your spine: push the floor away with your hands, tuck your chin and your pelvis.
  4. Keep the rhythm slow and even, one breath per cycle.

The mistake to avoid

Moving only your head and lower back. Your neck drops, your lower back arches, and the whole middle of your back stays frozen. Try to roll your entire spine, one vertebra after another, from your pelvis to your neck. The upper back, between the shoulder blades, is often the stiffest part, and it's the one that matters for the line.
