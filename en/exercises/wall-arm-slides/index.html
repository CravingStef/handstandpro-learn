Wall arm slides

Back to the wall, arms overhead, forearms against the wall from elbow to wrist. You slide your arms down, then back up, without anything coming off the wall, neither your back nor your forearms.

The point is that the wall won't let you cheat. In a handstand, a lack of shoulder mobility hides in an arch: your arms can't get through, so your back bends to finish the line. Here, the wall stops you from compensating, and you see exactly where your range stops.

How to do it

  1. Back to the wall, lower back and upper back touching it.
  2. Raise your arms overhead, forearms flat on the wall.
  3. Slide your arms down the wall, trying to get your elbows to 90 degrees.
  4. Slide back up slowly, keeping contact with the wall.

The mistake that skews everything

Arching your back to go lower. Your lower back leaves the wall, your rib cage opens, and your arms gain a few centimeters. It feels like progress, but it's exactly the compensation the handstand will make you do: the banana. Only go as low as the contact allows, even if that seems like very little. If your forearms come off the wall before your elbows reach 90 degrees, that's your range for now, and it's useful information.

My limit, measured at the wall

Shoulder mobility is my limit in handstand. I said so publicly as early as July 2026, and it's decent but could be better. I added this drill to the app on 19 September 2026. Of the three shoulder drills I recommend on the mobility page, it's the least spectacular, but it's the one that shows me where my range really stops, without my back stepping in to fix things.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your back comes off the wall to go further:

“Keep your lower back on the wall, don't arch to go lower”
