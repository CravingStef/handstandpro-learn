Wall plank

The first time your shoulders carry your weight overhead. Hands on the floor, feet on the wall at hip height, you stay low and can put your feet back down at any moment. You don't need to have been upside down before.

How to do it

  1. Get on all fours with your back to the wall, soles of your feet against it.
  2. Put one foot on the wall, then the other, up to about hip height.
  3. Press your feet into the wall and brace your belly: your body becomes a plank.
  4. Shift your weight toward your hands and push the floor away. Hold.
  5. To finish, step down one foot at a time.

What makes the wall plank fail

Leaving your weight in your feet. Your feet on the wall are there to steady you, not to carry you. If you mostly feel your legs, move your shoulders a little further over your hands: that's where the drill starts.

A sagging belly. Your hips drop, your back arches, and the load goes into your lower back instead of your shoulders. Brace your belly and glutes like in a floor plank, and keep your hips in line.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your legs start doing all the work:

“Bias your weight onto your hands”
