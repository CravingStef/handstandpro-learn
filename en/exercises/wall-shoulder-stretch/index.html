Wall shoulder stretch

Hands flat on the wall above your head, you walk your feet back until your back is flat, your torso roughly parallel to the floor. Then you let your chest sink gently between your arms, and you hold.

In a handstand, your arms have to come in line with your body, ears between your arms. When your shoulders don't open enough, your arms stay in front and your back arches to finish the line, the famous banana. This stretch works directly on that opening, and all you need is a wall. Down dog works on it from the floor, with part of your weight on your hands.

How to do it

  1. Facing the wall, place your hands flat on it above your head, then walk your feet back until your back is flat.
  2. Round your upper back slightly to keep your lower back neutral.
  3. Gently push your shoulders down, arms staying overhead, ears between your arms.
  4. Hold the position and breathe slowly.

Two mistakes to avoid

Arching your lower back to go lower. Your belly pushes forward, your ribs flare, and your chest gains a few centimeters. Those centimeters come from your back arching, the exact compensation you want to avoid upside down. Tuck your ribs in and accept not going as low.

Forcing it all at once. A shoulder pushed abruptly to the end of its range tenses up and won't let go. Go down gradually, breathing, and gain range little by little from one breath to the next.

The one I used to skip

At the end of July 2026, on the mobility page, I wrote that if I had to keep only one shoulder drill, it would be this stretch, chest sinking between the arms (on a table or a chair in that version). I added that it was also the one I quietly skipped when I was short on time, and that this probably explained why my shoulders were still holding my line back.

Shoulder opening is something I'm still trying to improve. It can be trained, and a wall is enough: if you can only keep one stretch in a rushed session, keep this one.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your back arches so you can go lower:

“Ribs in, don't let your back arch”
