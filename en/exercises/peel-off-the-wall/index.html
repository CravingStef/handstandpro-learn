Peel off the wall

You're in a handstand with your back to the wall. One foot leaves the wall, gently, and you look for where the balance is. Then the second foot joins it, and there's nothing behind you anymore: your fingers and palms are doing the holding.

In heel pulls, both heels leave together and come back to touch. Here you go through an in-between stage, one foot still on the wall, the other already off it, which gives you time to feel the balance point before you settle into it.

The app gives it to you throughout the freestanding branch, from the first split holds all the way to the one-minute goal. It's the drill that bridges the wall and everything after it.

How to do it

  1. Kick up into a handstand with your back to the wall, feet resting against it, core engaged.
  2. Gently peel one foot off the wall.
  3. Feel out where the balance point is, pressing less and less with the other foot.
  4. Peel the second foot off to bring it together with the first.
  5. Push through your fingers or palms to correct, and push tall through your shoulders.

Three sets of three, in the app. If you tip toward the wall, it catches you: that margin for error is what makes the drill useful.

Two mistakes to avoid

Kicking off the wall. A foot that pushes the wall to leave it sends you past the balance point, toward your hands, and you fall forward without having felt anything. Your foot leaves the wall because you press less, not because you push it away.

Correcting with your legs. When you start to tip, the reflex is to bend at the hips or spread your legs. That saves you once, then the line is gone. Corrections happen in your hands: your fingers when you drift toward the wall, your palms when you drift away from it.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your second foot leaves the wall:

“Slow and smooth, no sudden moves”
