Kick-tuck to full

You kick straight up into a tuck, knees pulled toward your chest, and hold it for a beat. Then you slowly extend your legs to a full handstand, and come back to the tuck the same way before coming down.

As your legs unfold, your center of gravity rises and moves away from your hands, and your shoulders have to open to keep your hips over them. A lack of shoulder opening shows up here straight away. Learn the starting position at the wall first, with the wall tuck hold.

How to do it

  1. Kick straight up into a tuck, knees toward your chest, and hold it for a beat.
  2. Push hard through your hands, hips over your shoulders.
  3. Slowly extend your legs to a full handstand.
  4. Fold your legs back into the tuck, still slowly.
  5. Come down with control.

The two mistakes that make you fall

Letting your hips move as you extend your legs. Your legs go up, your hips go with them, and you lose the stack you had in the tuck. Keep your hips over your shoulders from start to finish: your legs unfold around them.

Going too fast. A quick extension goes through the full handstand without stopping there, and a quick fold makes you lose your balance. The movement is slow in both directions, and that slowness is what forces you to stay balanced at every moment.

Still in my sessions

In early August 2026, the tuck was one of the three things I listed to get back, along with my sense of balance and shoulder opening. I still work on it, sometimes freestanding, sometimes against a wall, because it trains shoulder opening, and that's something I'm still trying to improve.

It didn't stop me from holding over a minute with my feet together at the end of October 2026. You can make progress on the full handstand while the tuck is coming along, and keep this drill in your sessions to work on your shoulders.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when you extend your legs:

“Keep your hips stacked over your shoulders throughout”
