Wall pike hold

Body folded at a right angle, feet on the wall at hip height, hands on the floor. From the side it draws a 7. This is where your shoulders start carrying for real, without you having to go fully upside down.

It has one thing the wall plank does not: your hips pass above your shoulders. Your upper body is already vertical, and your shoulders find out what that asks of them.

How to do it

  1. Sit facing the wall, legs straight, soles of your feet against it.
  2. Turn around and put your hands exactly where you were sitting. That's your distance, nothing to measure.
  3. Walk your feet up the wall to about hip height.
  4. Straight arms, ears between them, push the floor away.
  5. Bring your shoulders right over your hands, hips just above.
  6. To come out, walk your feet back down the wall.

The two mistakes that empty the drill

Keeping your shoulders behind your hands. That's the comfortable version, and it's also the one where nothing works: the weight stays in your feet, so in the wall. Move your shoulders forward until you feel the weight tip into your hands. If you feel nothing in your shoulders, they aren't far enough forward.

Letting your back arch. Your feet push, your hips travel back, your ribs flare. The position looks taller, and the line you are rehearsing is the wrong one. Pull your ribs in, brace your belly, and accept dropping your feet a few centimeters if that's the price.

This is where my limit shows

My shoulder opening is my real limit, and I have said so publicly since I started writing about this. Decent, but improvable. In this position it has nowhere to hide: when the opening isn't there, the shoulders refuse to pass over the hands and the back compensates by arching. This is the drill where I can see whether I have made progress, without a stopwatch.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your elbows start to give:

“Straight arms, don't bend the elbows”
