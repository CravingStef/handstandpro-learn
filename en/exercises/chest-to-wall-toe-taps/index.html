Chest-to-wall toe taps

The chest-to-wall hold, with a game added. Only your toes touch the wall, and you switch which one does: one foot comes off, the other stays, then they trade places. Between two taps there's a brief moment where nothing is holding you, and that's the moment this drill tries to stretch out.

It comes after toe pulls. Since one foot always stays close to the wall, you can chain attempts without coming down.

How to do it

  1. Walk up until your chest faces the wall. Only your toes touch it.
  2. Stack your hips over your shoulders and push tall through your shoulders.
  3. Slowly lift one toe off and feel the balance.
  4. Put it back and lift the other. To keep from drifting too far from the wall, press hard through your fingers.
  5. With each attempt, let a little more time pass between one toe leaving and the other coming back.

Two mistakes to avoid

Resting on the wall. If one foot keeps pressing its full weight into the wall while the other moves, the wall is balancing for you. Your toes brush the wall, they don't carry anything. When you feel yourself drifting away from it, pressing through your fingertips is what brings you back.

Arching your back on every tap. The foot that comes off drags your hips with it, and your lower back arches to compensate. Think hollow body, ribs in, and settle for a smaller foot movement.

My other favorite drill

This and the split handstand are my two favorite drills, and I do this one with my hands placed fairly far from the wall. What took me the longest in handstand (and still does) is judging how much energy to put into small corrections: too much push through the fingers and you tap back into the wall, not enough and you drift away from it. Alternating toe taps train exactly that, dozens of times per set, with the wall there to catch every mistake.

Your brain learns this as much as your shoulders do. It means building new connections, and that comes with repetition. A few minutes of taps every time you train, and the right correction ends up happening without you thinking about it.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your toes stay resting on the wall:
