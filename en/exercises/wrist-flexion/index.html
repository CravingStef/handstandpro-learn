Wrists · Warm-up

Wrist flexion

Your hands are turned backward, fingers toward your knees, palms on the floor. You lean your shoulders back slowly and feel the front of the wrist stretch. It's the usual warm-up move, in reverse.

Everything in a handstand loads the wrist in one direction, hand flat and forearm tipping forward. That direction gets worked without you thinking about it, every time you put your hands down. The other one never gets anything unless you go looking for it. That's the whole point of this position, and it's also why it surprises you the first time.

How to do it

  1. Kneel down and place your hands turned backward, palms flat, fingers toward your knees.
  2. Arms straight, elbows locked.
  3. Lean your shoulders back very gradually: that lean is what loads the front of the wrist.
  4. Come back as soon as it pulls hard, then go again a little less far.

The two mistakes to avoid

Going for range straight away. This direction gets worked far less often than the other one, and it lets you know. Lean all the way to your limit on day one and you'll feel it in your next session. Go to where you feel a solid stretch, no further, and let the range come over the weeks.

Bending your elbows to go further. Your torso still moves back, but the load goes around the wrist instead of through it. Keep your arms straight and accept less range: it's the only way the work lands where it should.

Both directions, not one

I do rotations and flexions before every session, and they are two different directions, not two versions of the same drill. Here again, the exact drill doesn't matter much: there's a wide variety of wrist preparations, and one is as good as another as long as it turns the joint or builds its flexibility.

What counts is bringing the load on gradually before you start. Two or three minutes is enough, and it's the best investment in your whole session.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when you want to force your way further:
