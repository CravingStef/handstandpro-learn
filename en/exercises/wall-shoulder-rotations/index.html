Shoulders · At the wall

Wall shoulder rotations

Facing the wall, about 30 cm away, arms up and hands flat on it. One hand slides down and passes under the opposite arm, staying on the wall, to the end of your range. Then it slides back up, and you switch sides.

For your arms to line up overhead, the shoulder doesn't just raise the arm: it also has to rotate. Wall arm slides train the arm going up. This one trains the rotation, the other half of shoulder opening.

How to do it

  1. Facing the wall, 30 cm away, raise your arms and place your hands flat on it.
  2. Slide one hand down and under the opposite arm, without leaving the wall.
  3. Go to the end of your range, then slide back up, slowly and with control.
  4. Switch sides and repeat.

Two mistakes to avoid

Turning your whole body. To go further, your torso turns and follows your hand, and your spine does the work instead of your shoulder. Keep your chest facing the wall, even if it means stopping sooner.

Taking your hand off the wall. A hand that leaves the wall goes wherever it likes, and your shoulder no longer works against anything. Keep your hand on the wall the whole way, and gain range little by little, from one rep to the next.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your torso turns to go further:

“Keep your chest square, don't turn the whole body”
