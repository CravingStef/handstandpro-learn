Cartwheel bail

The exit you'll use on every freestanding attempt. When the handstand goes past vertical, one hand steps to the side, your hips turn, and you land on your feet like in a cartwheel, only lower and softer.

Until it's automatic, a protective reflex will keep your kick-ups too timid, and you won't understand why you always come down on the same side.

How to do it

  1. Get up on the wall the way you usually do, chest to wall or with a kick-up.
  2. Step one hand out to the side, then shift your weight onto it.
  3. Turn your hips and let your legs come down to the side.
  4. Put your feet down one after the other, controlling the descent.
  5. Repeat on the same side until it's automatic, then move away from the wall.

The mistakes that keep coming back

Bridging out. Dropping forward in an arc with your back arched looks spectacular, but it's hard on the spine and useless as a reflex. The cartwheel goes to the side.

Landing heavily. If your feet slap the floor, the descent isn't controlled. Aim for a quiet landing: it's the simplest way to know the exit is solid.

You don't need a real cartwheel, by the way. The exit is a low half cartwheel: if you can step one foot down to the side from the wall, you can do it.

Learned before the fear

I learned the cartwheel bail as a child, at a gymnastics club, and I don't remember a single bad fall on it. I recommend the same order to everyone: the exit first, then freestanding.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your feet land too hard:
