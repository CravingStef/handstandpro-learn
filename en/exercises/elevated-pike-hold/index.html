Elevated pike hold

The same right angle as at the wall, but with your feet on something. A step, a bench, a sofa, a moving box. The point is the adjustment: every extra centimeter of height brings the position closer to a handstand and moves weight into your hands.

It's the drill that replaces the wall when you want to pick your own dose of vertical.

How to do it

  1. Put your feet on the support, hands on the floor in front of you.
  2. Walk your hands in, step by step, until your hips are above your shoulders.
  3. Straight arms, push tall through your shoulders, and hold the line from hands to hips.
  4. Lift your hips as high as you can over your hands, belly braced.
  5. Your eyes land between your hands.
  6. To come out, walk your hands back a step and put a knee down.

The two mistakes that break the line

Sinking into your shoulders. Your arms hold, but your shoulders drop toward the floor and your head sinks between them. The weight crushes into your wrists. Push as if to make yourself taller, neck long.

Picking a support that's too low. Feet twenty centimeters off the floor and it's still an inclined plank: your hips never pass above your shoulders and nothing gets loaded. Go up to hip height, or find something taller.

I was missing a piece of furniture at the right height

After my record in February 2023, the next step asked me to get upside down with one foot resting at hip height. I had no box, no board, no low wall at that height where I trained. It's one of the reasons my practice stopped that year, and it was neither an injury nor boredom. I was missing a piece of furniture. That's why I take the time to say here that a stair step or a sofa will do the job.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your eyes drift toward your feet:

“Look at your hands, not at your feet”
