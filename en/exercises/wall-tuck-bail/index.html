Wall tuck bail

The gentlest way to learn how to come down. From a tucked position against the wall, one hand steps away, then a foot, then the other, and you're back on the floor without ever falling.

You learn it before going high: fear of falling mostly comes from not knowing how to get down.

How to do it

  1. Facing the wall, get into a tuck: knees toward your chest, feet against the wall, hips over your hands.
  2. Stay low and tightly tucked. Don't try to straighten your legs.
  3. Move one hand out to the side, and let your body turn toward it.
  4. Put one foot down, then the other, softly.
  5. Always repeat on the same side, until the movement happens without thinking.

Two mistakes to avoid

Thinking before you move. The longer you wait at the top, the more you tense up, and the harder the descent gets. The exit is decided before you go up: you know which side you'll come out on, and you go.

Switching sides every time. At first, one side only, always the same one. Repetition is what turns it into a reflex, and a reflex doesn't build on two sides at once.

What I saw in class

In group classes, I watched people come down hard, landing heavily on their toes and ankles, because nobody had shown them the way down. That's when I understood that coming down is a skill, and that it's about injury before it's about fear.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when you freeze at the top:

“Minimal thinking, just step down”
