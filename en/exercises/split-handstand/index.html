Split handstand

The handstand without a wall, one leg forward and one back, like scissors. Your center of gravity sits lower than with your feet together, which makes it the best place to learn freestanding balance.

Not to be confused with the straddle, legs wide to the sides.

How to do it

  1. Stand in a lunge, raise your arms and place your hands on the floor in front of you.
  2. Kick your back leg up, without trying to bring your feet together.
  3. Lock your legs in a scissor, one forward, one back.
  4. Find the point where your hips are over your hands, and hold it with your fingers.
  5. When you go past vertical, cartwheel out.

Two mistakes to avoid

Letting fear set the kick. You only learn the right kick by getting it wrong both ways: too hard one time, too soft the next. If you always drop back to the same side, fear is often holding the kick back, and the cartwheel bail is what fixes that.

A wandering gaze. Your head tucking between your arms, or reaching for the floor, shifts your balance every time. Pick a spot at the base of your hands and stay on it.

My favorite drill

It's one of my two favorite drills, along with alternating toe taps at the wall. I held 57 seconds freestanding on 13 August 2026, after 23 seconds filmed in Corsica on 29 July.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when your back starts to arch:

“Tilt your pelvis backward”
