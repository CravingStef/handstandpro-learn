Wrists · Warm-up

Wrist rocks

Circles and rotations move your wrists with no weight on them. Rocks are the next step: kneeling, hands on the floor, you shift part of your bodyweight onto your wrists by moving your shoulders past your hands, then you come back. Each rep, you go a little further.

It's the same position as the handstand, wrists in extension, but with a fraction of the weight and the option to back off at any moment. The last preparation before you go up.

How to do it

  1. Kneel with your palms flat on the floor under your shoulders, fingers spread.
  2. Shift your shoulders past your wrists, then rock back.
  3. To come back, push through your fingertips.
  4. Add a little range each rep.

Once you're comfortable, move on to the opposite stretch, wrist flexion: hands flipped, fingers toward your knees, and this time you move your shoulders back.

The mistake that hurts

Going straight to the end of your range. The first rock goes as far as the tenth, your wrists suddenly take an angle and a load they aren't ready for, and you feel it pull. The whole point of the drill is to build the load in steps. Start with a small movement, almost nothing, and let the range come by itself.

Forearms that ached

During one comeback to handstand, a few years ago, I had mild pain in my wrists, and in my forearms too. It went away little by little with practice, as long as I didn't force it. In late July 2026, I had to stop for nearly a week, wrists and right hand tired, and that time it was my own fault: I was rushing my warm-ups.

I don't much mind which drill you pick, there are dozens. What I care about is building the load gradually before putting all my weight on my hands, and that's exactly what this one does.

A coach in your ear

Upside down, you can't read a screen. In the app, a voice guides you through the exercise and tells you what to fix right when you need it. For example, when you go straight to the end of your range:

“Load your wrists gradually”
