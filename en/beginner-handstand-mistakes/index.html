The 7 beginner handstand mistakes (and how to fix them)

Each of these mistakes costs weeks, sometimes months. None of them is inevitable: they all have an identifiable cause and a precise fix. In order of frequency.

1. The banana (arched back, legs drifting behind)

The symptom: seen from the side, your body draws an arc, belly forward, legs falling behind your head.
The real cause: almost never weak abs. It's either insufficient shoulder opening (the body compensates for closed shoulders by arching the lower back) or a missing shoulder push. Test your shoulders in 30 seconds.
The fix: chest-to-wall holds, which make the banana physically impossible, plus shoulder-opening work. Fixing the banana while freestanding never works: you can't rewrite a position while surviving it.

2. Balancing with the hips

The symptom: big body waves, pedaling legs, a visible struggle.
The real cause: the fingers aren't doing their job. Handstand balance is 80% finger pressure into the floor, like your toes when you stand upright.
The fix: toe pulls chest-to-wall (drill 4 of the wall progression), where you learn finger braking 15 cm from a safety net.

3. Not knowing how to exit

The symptom: all your kick-ups fall back the way they came, never past vertical.
The real cause: your brain deliberately caps your momentum as long as it doesn't know an emergency exit. It's not clumsiness, it's self-protection.
The fix: 2 sessions of cartwheel bail out. The best return on investment in the entire learning process. The full tutorial.

4. Looking too far forward (or tucking the head)

The symptom: head craned up, breaking the neck line, or chin glued to the chest, "hidden head".
The real cause: nobody told you where to look.
The fix: gaze lands on the floor, between the hands or just slightly ahead of their line. Enough to stabilize the inner ear, not enough to arch the neck.

5. The forced kick-up

The symptom: an explosive entry, a chaotic pass through vertical, a rushed exit. Every attempt looks different from the last.
The real cause: believing momentum can compensate for lack of control.
The fix: kick with 70% of the force you think you need, hands already on the floor before the kick (no diving). A short kick is rescued by shifting the shoulders slightly forward; a strong kick can only be rescued by exiting.

6. Training at the end of a workout

The symptom: handstand practice after lifting or running, "to make the trip worth it", with erratic results.
The real cause: the handstand is precision motor learning. Tired, you repeat degraded patterns, and that's what your nervous system records.
The fix: handstands are trained fresh, at the start of a session or in a dedicated 15-minute slot. Ideal frequency detailed here.

7. Measuring nothing

The symptom: "I'm stuck", said without evidence, followed by quitting between month 2 and month 4.
The real cause: handstand progress is real but imperceptible by feel. Going from 1 to 3 seconds is tripling, and yet it doesn't "feel" like anything.
The fix: time every attempt and log the best hold of each session. Progress is almost always there, and seeing it is enough to keep going. That's exactly what the skill path in Handstand Pro is for. To place your times against classic milestones: the benchmarks by level.

The common thread of these 7 mistakes

Notice the pattern: none of these mistakes is fixed by "trying harder". Each is fixed by changing the context (the wall, the bail out, the fresh session, the timer). That's the great lesson of the handstand: when you're stuck, it's not effort that needs increasing, it's the method that needs adjusting.

FAQ

Which mistake is the most expensive?
Number 3. Not knowing how to exit mechanically blocks every other progression, and it's fixed in 2 sessions.

How do I know if I'm doing the banana?
Film yourself from the side. Sensation systematically lies upside down: most people in a banana believe they're straight.

I'm making several of these mistakes, where do I start?
In order: the exit (#3), then wall alignment (#1), then the fingers (#2). The rest adjusts along the way.
