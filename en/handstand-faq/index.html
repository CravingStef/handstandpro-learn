Handstand: the complete FAQ

Short answers to the questions we get most, about handstand practice and about the Handstand Pro app. For the big topics (learning, the wall, fear, timelines), each answer links to the detailed article.

The practice

Does the handstand build muscle?
Yes, mainly the shoulders, traps, forearms and deep core, through isometric work. It won't replace lifting for building size, but it develops remarkable shoulder strength and core control, and it complements a classic training program very well.

How many calories does a handstand burn?
Few on its own: a handful of calories per minute of holding. The point of the handstand isn't energy expenditure, it's strength, motor control and mobility. A full 20-minute session is still a genuine physical effort, though.

Are handstands and lifting compatible?
Perfectly, with one rule: handstands are trained fresh, before lifting or in a separate slot, never after. A fatigued nervous system records bad patterns. (Why, in detail)

Are handstand push-ups the same thing?
No. The handstand push-up is a strength exercise (push-ups in a vertical position, usually against a wall); the handstand is a balance skill. You can be strong at wall handstand push-ups and unable to hold 2 freestanding seconds, and vice versa.

Can you practice while pregnant?
Inversions during pregnancy are an individual medical decision: talk to your doctor or midwife before any practice. Many experienced practitioners adapt early in pregnancy and then pause; it's not the time to start learning handstands if you're a beginner.

Do I need equipment or a mat?
No. A smooth wall, 2 meters of clear, firm floor, and that's it. In fact, avoid thick mats and cushions: they shift under your hands and hinder more than they protect. Real safety is knowing how to come down. (The cartwheel bail out)

Barefoot or in shoes?
Barefoot (or socks off) preferably: bare toes grip the wall better and alignment sensations are finer. Shoes neither help nor hurt in freestanding balance, it's a comfort choice.

Blood rushing to my head, dizziness, is that normal?
A feeling of pressure in the head during the first weeks is common and fades with habituation, especially since beginner holds last a few seconds. Marked dizziness, persistent headaches or vision changes are not normal: stop and consult. With hypertension, glaucoma or cardiovascular conditions, get medical advice before starting. (Precautions after 30 or 40)

From what age can a child learn?
Children learn naturally from age 6 to 8, ideally in a supervised setting (kids' gymnastics). Our method and our app are designed for adults.

How does the skill path work?
The app guides you drill by drill, from wrist warm-ups to freestanding balance, through the wall work and the "Cartwheel bail out" module. Each skill unlocks when the previous standard is met, and today's session is generated for your level.

How does the app track my progress?
A built-in timer records your hold times and keeps your best times, session after session. That's what makes visible a progress that sensation alone can't perceive.
